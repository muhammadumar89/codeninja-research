# Physical AI for maritime and ports: questions answered

Canonical: https://codeatoms.ai/sectors/maritime-and-ports/
License: CC BY 4.0
Publisher: CodeNinja Atoms (https://codeatoms.ai)

Terminals and port authorities run gates, cranes, yards and finance on separate systems, so nobody sees the whole flow of a box or a truck.

## What does a physical AI system for maritime and ports look like?

A complete physical AI design for maritime and ports names what to sense, which existing systems to join, the object model that joins them, the models and hardware, the three-year cost and the person who approves every action. CodeNinja Atoms has published 3 such reference architectures for maritime and ports, each free to reuse under CC BY 4.0. Port Twin (United States): one authoritative digital twin that binds a port's operational systems, live sensor feeds and finance backbone into a thirteen-object ontology, self-hosted on the port's own virtual machines inside the continental United States. Terminal Pulse (United States): truck turn time predicted two hours out and yard congestion seen live, at a container terminal, on the operator's own hardware with no outbound connection. Port Drone Watch (Germany): An open reference architecture for system design in physical AI, built for a maritime and ports authority in Germany that already flies a docked drone network across two port sites, where owning the inference hardware that runs it costs about 735,000 US dollars over three years. Every flight's imagery becomes detections, and every detection becomes a work item with a named responder role, anchored to a real berth, quay asset or restricted zone in a living map built from the port's own spatial data. Oil sheen, floating debris, persons in restricted zones, fender or bollard damage, smoke or vapour and works progress reach a person who decides, under the European specific category drone rules, the Seveso regime and GDPR.

## Which AI models can a maritime and ports operator run on its own hardware?

Each published maritime and ports design names its models and why, and every model is open-weight or no model is used at all, so the operator can run it on hardware it owns. Port Twin: One self-hosted open model, BGE-M3, for document retrieval; no generative model and no token stream. Terminal Pulse: 5 self-hosted open models: GLM 5.3 (reasoning), Chronos-2 (forecasting), Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B (retrieval), RF-DETR (vision), Roboflow trackers. Port Drone Watch: RF-DETR (Apache-2.0) for per-frame detection at the dock; OWLv2 (Apache-2.0) for zero-shot passes on oil sheen, debris, smoke and damage; Ministral 3 8B (Apache-2.0) for evidence triage and work item drafting; Roboflow trackers (Apache-2.0) for motion and association; GLM 5.3 (bespoke licence, 753B at FP8) for the agentic work surface; nothing trained into weights.

## How much compute and hardware does AI in maritime and ports need?

The compute follows from the models: the published maritime and ports designs size it as follows, from no new hardware to a full GPU node. Port Twin, compute: No equipment bought: about 1.1 GB of fp16 weights on the port's existing enterprise virtual machines. Port Twin, boundary: Everything runs inside the continental United States on infrastructure the port already operates; no hosted document AI service in the path. Terminal Pulse, frontier compute: One node of eight 141 GB HBM-class GPUs holds GLM 5.3 at FP8 (753 GB of weights, 904 GB with headroom), at about 10 kW. Terminal Pulse, edge: Fanless IP-rated enclosures at the yard blocks, gate and quay; no safety reflex crosses a network hop. Port Drone Watch, compute: Three tiers on the authority's own hardware: a GPU-class edge box at each dock, sized at the dock site survey; a site server at each port site with one to four 48 GB PCIe cards; and one node of eight 141 GB HBM cards (1,128 GB) in the port's own data centre, holding 753 GB of FP8 weights with a 1.2 planning factor, 904 GB. Detection events and evidence crops cross the boundary; pixels stay at the dock.

## Is it cheaper to own AI hardware or rent cloud GPUs in maritime and ports?

Each maritime and ports design prices three years of ownership in its Appendix A, with every price cited, against renting the same capacity from a cloud region at its deepest three-year commitment where hardware is bought. Port Twin: three-year cost, No hardware line to price: the design adds software and integration work to servers the port already runs. Terminal Pulse: three-year cost, owned, About 722,000 US dollars with support and power at the US industrial power price; three-year cost, rented, 0.99 million to 2.52 million US dollars for the same GPUs around the clock; ownership is about four fifths the deepest three-year commitment (version 2); closed model break-even, The cheapest closed model matches the owned stack at about 35 users; above that, ownership is cheaper and the gap grows with every user. Port Drone Watch: three-year cost, About 735,000 US dollars to own the central node and the two site servers for three years (a range of 650,000 to 845,000: servers, support and power at the Eurostat non household tariff for Germany), against about 761,000 to rent the same card classes on the deepest three-year commitment in AWS Europe (Stockholm for the eight-card node, which Frankfurt does not sell, and Frankfurt for the site cards), so ownership costs about the same there, and about 2.02 million on demand, against which ownership costs about a third. Renting is a price reference only, because the imagery, the weights and the detection history stay on hardware the port owns; dock edge boxes are priced by quotation after the survey (Appendix A).

## What ontology or object model does a maritime and ports AI system need?

The object model is the part that makes the system an ontology rather than a pipeline: typed objects for the things in the maritime and ports operation, with properties, status values and typed links. Every published design ships its object model as hyper-ontology/1 JSON that loads into Hyper Ontology. Port Twin: Berth, Navigation channel, Subsurface utility layer, Environmental sensor feed, Parcel / facility, Bathymetric survey surface, Utility gap record, Capital project, Inspection record, Vessel movement record, Truck movement record, PCS message, Engineering document. Terminal Pulse: Vessel Call, Container, Yard Block, RTG, Ship to Shore Crane, Truck Visit, Gate Lane, Rail Cut, Reefer Plug, Yard Person, Transfer Zone, Safety Event. Port Drone Watch: Mission, Construction Work, Detection, Work Item, Flight Authorisation, Coverage Statement, Berth, Dock Station, Restricted Zone, Vessel, Quay Asset, Drone, Responder Role, Drone Operator, Weather Observation.

## Who approves the decisions an AI system makes in maritime and ports?

In every published maritime and ports design a named person makes the decision that changes the physical world; the system prepares it. Port Twin: The twin shows; pilots, berth planners, engineers and finance staff decide in their own systems, and nothing writes back into a system of record. Terminal Pulse: Surfaces warn and propose; the named planner acts in the terminal operating system and the named safety supervisor acknowledges every safety event. Port Drone Watch: The system proposes and records and people decide every response: a detection stays proposed until a responder confirms, rejects or merges it, every work item is acknowledged with reason codes by its named responder role, flight approvers grant every mission, and automatic acts are limited to confirmation re-flights and notification escalation.

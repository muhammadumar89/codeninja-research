# Physical AI for semiconductors: questions answered

Canonical: https://codeatoms.ai/sectors/semiconductors/
License: CC BY 4.0
Publisher: CodeNinja Atoms (https://codeatoms.ai)

A chip is specified, implemented, verified and handed to a foundry across separate tools, so the trace from a requirement to the check that proves it is usually rebuilt by hand.

## What does a physical AI system for semiconductors look like?

A complete physical AI design for semiconductors names what to sense, which existing systems to join, the object model that joins them, the models and hardware, the three-year cost and the person who approves every action. CodeNinja Atoms has published 2 such reference architectures for semiconductors, each free to reuse under CC BY 4.0. Silicon Ledger (Saudi Arabia): An open reference architecture for system design in physical AI, built for a fabless chip design company in Saudi Arabia that designs silicon in vendor design tool suites and hands tapeouts to an outside foundry, where owning the inference node that runs it costs about 569,000 US dollars over three years. Every requirement is traced to the block, interface and signal that implement it and to the check, test and coverage result that prove it, so the verification lead can ask which requirements have no proof and what one interface change on one block actually touches. The model runs entirely on the company's own hardware under the kingdom's data protection law, export control on the GPU node and trade secret law. Physical AI System Design for Wafer Defect Inspection in the United States (United States): An open reference architecture for physical AI system design, built for a high volume wafer fab in the United States that images wafers at many process steps with optical inspection and electron microscope review tools, where owning the reasoning node that runs it costs about 576,000 US dollars over three years. Defect images are classified at the tool, joined to the wafer, lot, tool, chamber and recipe in one governed fab ontology, and traced to an excursion and a hold, so the yield engineer can ask which chamber drifted, since when, and which lots it touched. The model runs entirely on the fab's own hardware under United States export control, including the deemed export rule for supporting engineers, and the sector's tool network cybersecurity and equipment communications standards.

## Which AI models can a semiconductor company run on its own hardware?

Each published semiconductors design names its models and why, and every model is open-weight or no model is used at all, so the operator can run it on hardware it owns. Silicon Ledger: GLM 5.3 (bespoke licence, 753B at FP8) for the agentic work surface: the spec agent, the check agent and cited answers; BGE-M3 (MIT) for embedding and retrieval so every answer cites its record; PaddleOCR-VL 1.6 (Apache-2.0) for specification parsing at ingestion; no third party model service, nothing trained into weights. Physical AI System Design for Wafer Defect Inspection in the United States: GLM 5.3 open weights for the work surface, self hosted inside the fab: reasoning for the three named agents and cited answers, unsized until a verified register row stands; an unsupervised anomaly model fitted on the fab's own normal images on the CodeNinja on-device AI stack at the tool; no third party model service, nothing trained into weights.

## How much compute and hardware does AI in semiconductors need?

The compute follows from the models: the published semiconductors designs size it as follows, from no new hardware to a full GPU node. Silicon Ledger, compute: One node of eight 141 GB HBM cards (1,128 GB) in the company's own server room, holding 753 GB of FP8 weights with a 1.2 planning factor, 904 GB, at roughly ten kilowatts; no edge estate. The node is a 4A090 item for a D:4 destination, so a BIS licence determination is a phase one checkpoint, and the platform runs on the company's existing hardware until it lands. Nothing crosses outward. Physical AI System Design for Wafer Defect Inspection in the United States, compute: At the tool, one octa core 6 TOPS NPU edge SoC unit per inspection tool group in a fanless sealed cleanroom enclosure, held to a 60 percent headroom gate. In the fab's server room, the 141 GB HBM GPU class, eight GPUs per node, one node for GLM 5.3 at FP8. Nothing crosses the fab perimeter.

## Is it cheaper to own AI hardware or rent cloud GPUs in semiconductors?

Each semiconductors design prices three years of ownership in its Appendix A, with every price cited, against renting the same capacity from a cloud region at its deepest three-year commitment where hardware is bought. Silicon Ledger: three-year cost, About 569,000 US dollars to own the node for three years (a range of 541,000 to 594,000: server, support and power at the Saudi commercial electricity tariff), against about 751,000 to rent the same eight cards on the deepest three-year commitment in AWS Middle East (UAE), so ownership costs about three quarters of it, and about 2.00 million on demand, against which ownership costs a little over a quarter. Renting is a price reference only, because AWS lists no Saudi region on the day read and every rented option would carry design records across the border (Appendix A). Physical AI System Design for Wafer Defect Inspection in the United States: three-year cost, About 576,000 US dollars to own the reasoning node for three years (a range of 551,000 to 623,000: server, support and power at the United States average industrial or commercial price), against about 625,000 to rent the same eight cards on the deepest three year commitment in AWS US East (N. Virginia), so ownership costs about nine tenths of it, and about 1.66 million on demand, against which ownership costs about a third. Renting is a price reference only, because every rented option would carry fab data out of the fab (Appendix A).

## What ontology or object model does a semiconductors AI system need?

The object model is the part that makes the system an ontology rather than a pipeline: typed objects for the things in the semiconductors operation, with properties, status values and typed links. Every published design ships its object model as hyper-ontology/1 JSON that loads into Hyper Ontology. Silicon Ledger: Specification section, Requirement, Design block, Interface, Signal, Design assumption, Verification check, Test, Coverage result, Bug, Waiver, Design revision, Engineer, Verification lead, Expert lesson, Foundry handoff. Physical AI System Design for Wafer Defect Inspection in the United States: Wafer, Lot, Process step, Tool, Chamber, Recipe, Inspection run, Defect, Defect class, Wafer map signature, Excursion, Hold, Corrective action, Defect review engineer, Yield engineer, Process owner.

## Who approves the decisions an AI system makes in semiconductors?

In every published semiconductors design a named person makes the decision that changes the physical world; the system prepares it. Silicon Ledger: Agents draft and engineers decide: an engineer confirms each extracted requirement before it is used, with the rejection rate as a phase one gate; the verification lead approves every drafted check before it enters the regression, escalating to the senior verification expert after five working days; a reconciliation job after every sync cycle flags stale impact lists and keeps them out of citations. Physical AI System Design for Wafer Defect Inspection in the United States: Agents draft and engineers decide: the defect review engineer confirms each proposed defect class, the yield engineer approves every drafted hold, and the process owner approves every release, rework or scrap; a hold placed or released without a recorded named approval is refused in code and audited per hold, and every supporting engineer is screened for deemed export before access.

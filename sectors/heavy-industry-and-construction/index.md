# Physical AI for heavy industry and construction: questions answered

Mills, factories and building sites produce counts, alarms and progress data in many systems, and each one sees only part of the operation.

## What does a physical AI system for heavy industry and construction look like?

A complete physical AI design for heavy industry and construction names what to sense, which existing systems to join, the object model that joins them, the models and hardware, the three-year cost and the person who approves every action. CodeNinja Atoms has published 5 such reference architectures for heavy industry and construction, each free to reuse under CC BY 4.0. Structure Phase Watch (Saudi Arabia): one live model of a construction site's structure phase that forecasts schedule slips three days out and shows safety breaches as they happen, on the contractor's own hardware inside the Kingdom. Steel Count Ledger (Pakistan): cameras and a GPU industrial PC at every casting strand and cooling bed count billets, ingots, rebars and girders as they are made, publish the counts through a one-way link into one record the authority owns, and let revenue officers reconcile counted against declared production. Factory Fire Watch (Saudi Arabia): live, read-only visibility of fire alarm panels, fire pumps, fire water tanks and energy meters across an operator's highest-risk factories, read through contacts, PLC inputs and LoRaWAN into an IoT platform hosted in Saudi Arabia, with never a write path into certified life-safety equipment. A Self-Improving AI System for Cement Kiln Control in Saudi Arabia (Saudi Arabia): An open reference architecture for system design in physical AI, built for a cement company in Saudi Arabia that runs several integrated plants, each with a preheater and precalciner kiln line of several thousand tonnes of clinker a day, where owning the inference node that runs it costs about 570,000 US dollars over three years. When a new alternative fuel lot drifts the burning zone over a shift, the design joins the streaming tags, the fuel lot, the raw meal, the laboratory results, the shell scan and the shift log into one argument, so the control room operator sees the likely cause, the recovery moves that worked in matching past upsets and the setpoint adjustments with their expected effect on thermal energy per tonne, and every confirmed outcome is kept as the memory the next shift reads. The models run entirely on the company's own hardware in the country under the national energy efficiency program's reporting, the environmental permit's emission limits, the national operational technology cybersecurity controls and the company's own management of change procedure. Industrial AI Reference Architecture for Fertilizer Plant Reliability in Pakistan (Pakistan): An open industrial AI reference architecture for system design in physical AI, built for a fertilizer manufacturer in Pakistan whose ammonia and urea complex, designed to run steadily between annual turnarounds, has been stopped and restarted repeatedly in 2026 by gas supply suspensions, where owning the inference node that runs it costs about 572,000 US dollars over three years. When the gas comes back after a two week suspension, the design joins the historian, the work orders, the inspection and corrosion records, the vibration readings and the shift log into one story per piece of equipment, so the restart agent drafts the plan from the last three restarts, the synthesis compressor's seal is flagged for inspection before the start, and the shift superintendent confirms every step. The models run entirely on the operator's own hardware inside the country, behind a hardware data diode that makes the read of the control system physically one way.

## Which AI models can a heavy industry and construction operator run on its own hardware?

Each published heavy industry and construction design names its models and why, and every model is open-weight or no model is used at all, so the operator can run it on hardware it owns. Structure Phase Watch: 5 self-hosted open models: GLM 5.3 (reasoning), Chronos-2 (forecasting), BGE-M3 (multilingual retrieval), RF-DETR (vision), Roboflow trackers. Steel Count Ledger: 3 self-hosted open models: RF-DETR (detection, fine-tuned per product type and mill), Roboflow trackers (identity across camera fields), GLM 5.3 (compliance work surface). Factory Fire Watch: One: IBM Granite Tiny Time Mixers (TTM-R2), about 0.85 million parameters, Apache-2.0, on CPU inside the platform; threshold evaluation on the safety path is rule logic. A Self-Improving AI System for Cement Kiln Control in Saudi Arabia: GLM 5.3 (GLM-5.3 bespoke licence, 753B at FP8) for cause drafting, handover drafting, ontology maintenance and the command centre's cited answers; Chronos-2 (Apache-2.0) forecasting the stable band on the streaming tags to raise the drift trigger; BGE-M3 (MIT) embedding past upsets, shift logs and maintenance history in Arabic and English; no third party model service, nothing trained into weights. Industrial AI Reference Architecture for Fertilizer Plant Reliability in Pakistan: GLM 5.3 (GLM-5.3 bespoke licence, 753B at FP8) for the restart, cycle damage and turnaround drafting agents and the cited conversational interface; Chronos-2 (Apache-2.0) forecasting historian tags and thermal cycle stress residuals on the site server; BGE-M3 (MIT) embedding work orders, findings, readings and shift log entries for cited retrieval; vision models on Frigate NVR and ONNX Runtime for steam plume, flange leak, operator, scaffold and fire or smoke; no third party model service, nothing trained into weights.

## How much compute and hardware does AI in heavy industry and construction need?

The compute follows from the models: the published heavy industry and construction designs size it as follows, from no new hardware to a full GPU node. Structure Phase Watch, frontier compute: One node of eight 141 GB HBM-class GPUs holds GLM 5.3 at FP8 (753 GB of weights, 904 GB with headroom). Structure Phase Watch, edge: Five Jetson Orin class nodes in solar powered enclosures at the gate, laydown, crane slew zone, batch plant and haul road. Steel Count Ledger, frontier compute: One node of eight 141 GB HBM-class GPUs holds GLM 5.3 at FP8 (753 GB of weights, 904 GB with headroom), in a dedicated data center behind an export licence checkpoint. Steel Count Ledger, edge: One sealed GPU industrial PC and IP66 HDR cameras per installation point; counting survives a slow wide-area link. Steel Count Ledger, the hard dependency: 141 GB-class accelerators need a US export licence for Pakistan (Country Group D:4); the node is ordered only once a licence naming the end user holds. Factory Fire Watch, compute: None bought: the requirement asks for no server or GPU, and the field gateways carry no model runtime. Factory Fire Watch, equipment, per factory: About 2,600 to 3,200 US dollars in list prices: a LoRaWAN gateway, two tank transmitters, two CT meters, four contact nodes, a switch, an enclosure and a UPS. A Self-Improving AI System for Cement Kiln Control in Saudi Arabia, compute: One node of eight 141 GB HBM accelerators (1,128 GB) in the company's own data centre in Saudi Arabia, holding 753 GB of FP8 weights with a 1.2 planning factor, 904 GB; a plant GPU server of one or two PCIe cards for the forecast and embedding models; a sealed fanless industrial edge unit at the plant. The node is a 4A090 class item for a D:3 and D:4 destination, so an export licence determination comes before the order, with two 80 GB class nodes as the fallback shape. Industrial AI Reference Architecture for Fertilizer Plant Reliability in Pakistan, compute: One node of eight 141 GB HBM cards (1,128 GB) on new dedicated in country hardware the operator owns, holding 753 GB of FP8 weights with a 1.2 planning factor, 904 GB; an air cooled site server inside the plant for Chronos-2, BGE-M3 and vision; a hardware data diode on the OT boundary and an OCP Time Card grandmaster for time. The node is a 4A090 class item for a destination in Country Groups D:2, D:3 and D:4, so an ECCN 3A090 and 4A090 licence determination is a phase one checkpoint before any card is quoted, with an approved in country data centre held as the fallback.

## Is it cheaper to own AI hardware or rent cloud GPUs in heavy industry and construction?

Each heavy industry and construction design prices three years of ownership in its Appendix A, with every price cited, against renting the same capacity from a cloud region at its deepest three-year commitment where hardware is bought. Structure Phase Watch: three-year cost, owned, About 642,000 US dollars with support and power at the Saudi industrial tariff; three-year cost, rented, 1.41 million to 2.81 million US dollars for the same GPUs around the clock; ownership is about one half the cheapest three-year commitment; closed model break-even, The cheapest closed model matches the owned stack at about 31 users; above that, ownership is cheaper and the gap grows with every user. Steel Count Ledger: three-year cost, owned, About 4,445,000 US dollars for 300 installation points, of which the counting kits are 2,832,000 and cannot be rented; owning the frontier node costs about 686,000 against 751,000 on AWS's deepest three-year plan; closed model break-even, The cheapest closed model matches the owned node at about 33 users; above that, ownership is cheaper. Factory Fire Watch: three-year cost, 100 factories, About 328,000 to 439,000 US dollars with support and power; the eight-factory pilot is about 21,000 to 26,000 of equipment. A Self-Improving AI System for Cement Kiln Control in Saudi Arabia: three-year cost, About 570,000 US dollars to own the frontier node for three years (a range of 542,000 to 594,000: server, support and power at the national commercial electricity tariff), against about 751,000 to rent the same eight cards on the deepest three year commitment in AWS Middle East (UAE), so ownership costs about three quarters of it, and about 2.00 million on demand, against which ownership costs a little over a quarter. Renting is a price reference only, because AWS lists no Saudi region on the day read and every rented option would carry plant data across the border (Appendix A). ## What ontology or object model does a heavy industry and construction AI system need?

The object model is the part that makes the system an ontology rather than a pipeline: typed objects for the things in the heavy industry and construction operation, with properties, status values and typed links. Every published design ships its object model as hyper-ontology/1 JSON that loads into Hyper Ontology. Structure Phase Watch: Tower crane (TC-01 to TC-14), Crawler crane (6 units), Casting bed, Precast unit, Flatbed or mixer truck, Batch ticket, P6 activity (owner baseline or fragnet), Permit to work, Incident or observation form, Gate movement, Delivery window, Lift schedule entry, Pour, Worker, Work zone (Zone A to D). Steel Count Ledger: Steel melting and re-rolling unit, Installation point, Industrial PC, Production count event, Product type (Billets, Ingots, Rebars, Girders), Declared production, Discrepancy case, Revenue field officer, Audit team, Steel mill staff, Tamper or offline alert, Daily uptime record, Product calibration record, Weighbridge record. Factory Fire Watch: High-risk factory, Fire alarm control panel, Fire pump, Fire water tank, Tank level transmitter, Energy meter (CT set), LoRaWAN gateway, Monitored point, Safety-critical alert, Audit log record, Periodic monitoring round, Preventive maintenance visit, Technical personnel, Monitoring officer. A Self-Improving AI System for Cement Kiln Control in Saudi Arabia: Kiln line, Equipment, Tag, Setpoint, Recipe, Raw meal lot, Fuel lot, Clinker sample and quality result, Upset, Operator action, Shell scan, Work order, Shift, Control room operator, Shift supervisor, Production manager. Industrial AI Reference Architecture for Fertilizer Plant Reliability in Pakistan: Process unit, Equipment, Historian tag, Trip, Restart, Thermal cycle, Work order, Inspection finding, Corrosion reading, Vibration reading, Lab result, Shift log entry, Turnaround task, Restart plan.

## Who approves the decisions an AI system makes in heavy industry and construction?

In every published heavy industry and construction design a named person makes the decision that changes the physical world; the system prepares it. Structure Phase Watch: Every re-sequencing is a recommendation a named planner approves; every breach alert is confirmed, dismissed or escalated by the HSE officer. Steel Count Ledger: Every discrepancy case is judged by a named revenue field officer; the system counts and reconciles, it never assesses, and the mill never edits a count. Factory Fire Watch: A monitoring officer acknowledges or escalates every safety-critical alert; the design monitors and never controls a panel, pump or valve. A Self-Improving AI System for Cement Kiln Control in Saudi Arabia: Agents draft and people decide, by name: the control room operator confirms the upset agent's cause and recovery draft, the shift supervisor approves the setpoint agent's draft, and the production manager confirms the shift agent's handover and its energy and quality comparison; drafts run in shadow before anyone approves them, and every confirmation or rejection is kept as the memory the next shift reads. Industrial AI Reference Architecture for Fertilizer Plant Reliability in Pakistan: Agents draft and people decide, by name: the shift superintendent confirms every step of the restart plan, the reliability engineer approves the cycle damage draft and the inspections it brings forward, the turnaround manager approves the scope additions, and report packs for the insurer's engineer leave only through a controlled export path after a disclosure review; every status change carries a named person and a timestamp, and every confirmation is kept as memory the next restart reads, never as a weight update.

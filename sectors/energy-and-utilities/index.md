# Physical AI for energy and utilities: questions answered

Canonical: https://codeatoms.ai/sectors/energy-and-utilities/
License: CC BY 4.0
Publisher: CodeNinja Atoms (https://codeatoms.ai)

Grids and plants run on control systems, maintenance records and field crews that were never designed to share one picture of risk.

## What does a physical AI system for energy and utilities look like?

A complete physical AI design for energy and utilities names what to sense, which existing systems to join, the object model that joins them, the models and hardware, the three-year cost and the person who approves every action. CodeNinja Atoms has published 8 such reference architectures for energy and utilities, each free to reuse under CC BY 4.0. Grid Context Watch (United States): one system of context that sees every device on a utility's control networks, flags abnormal behaviour and assembles the evidence a NERC CIP audit asks for, on the operator's own hardware. Reliability Atlas (Saudi Arabia): a records-based reliability and availability assessment that joins each plant's work orders, trip history and design documents into one reliability object model the operator owns, so every availability figure, criticality rank and root cause finding traces to its source. Feeder Firewatch (United States): a live ignition and outage risk score for every distribution feeder segment, built on the cooperative's own hardware, with every de-energisation and fast-trip change approved by a named operator. Turbine Island Watch (Pakistan): An open reference architecture for system design in physical AI. A condition monitoring and diagnostics platform for a rehabilitated 280 MW hydrogen-cooled steam turbine generator at a combined cycle plant in Pakistan: it reads the DCS, the vibration monitoring rack and the generator protection systems through a one-way path, ranks anomalies into prescribed actions for the shift operator, and runs entirely on the operator's own hardware. Sovereign AI Architecture for a Grid Control Room in Saudi Arabia (Saudi Arabia): An open reference architecture for system design in physical AI, built for a national grid operator in Saudi Arabia that runs the electricity transmission grid from regional and national control centers, where owning the inference node that runs it costs about 570,000 US dollars over three years. When one 380 kilovolt line trips at summer peak, the design joins the alarms, the network model, the outage plan, the protection record and the procedure library into one argument, so the control engineer sees which protection operated, what it affects, what work is already in progress nearby and which procedure governs the next step. The model runs entirely on the operator's own hardware under the national cybersecurity controls for operational technology, the grid code and export control on the GPU node. Industrial Data Ontology for a Water Utility in the United States (United States): An open reference architecture for system design in physical AI, built for a municipal water utility in the United States that runs a treatment plant, wells, a distribution reservoir, pump and lift stations and a pipe network under PLCs and RTUs, in the middle of a control system migration, where owning the inference server that runs it costs about 59,000 US dollars over three years. Every alarm tag is tied to the pump it measures, the station and pressure zone the pump serves, the work orders on it and the sampling points and permit limits on its zone, so the operator on shift can ask, the night a duty pump trips, which asset tripped, what work is open on it and what the permit requires of the zone it feeds. The model runs entirely on the utility's own hardware, reads the segregated control network only through a hardware data diode, and answers to the drinking water and discharge permit regime and the obligations of a state and local critical infrastructure owner. Sovereign AI Architecture for Distribution Loss and Theft Reduction in Pakistan (Pakistan): An open reference architecture for system design in physical AI, built for an electricity distribution company in Pakistan that serves several million customers through hundreds of grid stations and thousands of feeders, where owning the inference hardware that runs it costs about 641,000 US dollars over three years. When a transformer's monthly energy balance crosses its threshold, the design joins the smart meter interval load, the tamper events, the billing history, the network topology and the inspection record into one argument, so the subdivision engineer sees the ranked causes, the inspection officer opens a case file already populated with the load history and the billing record, and the revenue officer approves the correction. The models run entirely on the operator's own hardware in the country under the regulator's performance standards, the national data protection rules, the electricity theft laws and export control on the GPU node. A Self-Improving AI System for OT Vulnerability Management at a Utility in the United States (United States): An open reference architecture for system design in physical AI, built for an electric utility in the United States whose substations, control centers and generating plants fall under the critical infrastructure protection standards, where owning the inference node that runs it costs about 477,000 US dollars over three years. When a vendor publishes a relay firmware advisory on day thirty of a cycle, the design joins the device inventory, the verified baseline, the advisory, the national vulnerability record and the decisions made the last time this vendor's advisories touched these devices into one argument, so the compliance engineer sees which devices it touches and why, the asset owner approves apply or mitigate with its outage window and change request, and the auditor finds the evidence package where audits already look. The model runs entirely on the utility's own hardware, on open weights of United States origin, with nothing about its assets leaving its network.

## Which AI models can an energy and utilities operator run on its own hardware?

Each published energy and utilities design names its models and why, and every model is open-weight or no model is used at all, so the operator can run it on hardware it owns. Grid Context Watch: GLM 5.2 (MIT) for reasoning and Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B (Apache-2.0) for retrieval; detection stays in the procured sensors. Reliability Atlas: None: the register is empty by design because no inference workload is contracted. Feeder Firewatch: Self-hosted open-weight models: GLM 5.2 under MIT (reasoning) on site, RF-DETR (vision) at the edge. Turbine Island Watch: MOMENT-1 (MIT) for zero-shot anomaly detection over vibration, hydrogen, winding and process telemetry; IBM Granite 4.2 8B (Apache-2.0) for diagnostic notes and test-report drafts; no training run. Sovereign AI Architecture for a Grid Control Room in Saudi Arabia: GLM 5.3 (GLM-5.3 bespoke licence, 753B at FP8) for all four drafting agents (alarm triage, switching programs, handover and the sequence of events), the command center and ontology maintenance; BGE-M3 (MIT) for Arabic and English retrieval so every answer cites its procedure or record; no third party model service, nothing trained into weights. Industrial Data Ontology for a Water Utility in the United States: gpt-oss-120b (Apache-2.0, 117B, MXFP4) for the agentic work surface: the tag mapping agent, the alarm context agent, the monthly compliance drafting agent and cited answers; Nomic Embed Text v2 MoE (Apache-2.0) for retrieval so every answer cites its record; United States origin weights for a state and local critical infrastructure buyer; no third party model service, nothing trained into weights. Sovereign AI Architecture for Distribution Loss and Theft Reduction in Pakistan: GLM 5.3 (GLM-5.3 bespoke licence, 753B at FP8) for the loss, tamper and billing agents, the command center and ontology maintenance; BGE-M3 (MIT) for cited retrieval over tariff codes, meter numbers, PPC sections and inspection findings; Chronos-2 (Apache 2.0) forecasting each transformer's energy balance so the residual is the anomaly signal; no third party model service, nothing trained into weights. A Self-Improving AI System for OT Vulnerability Management at a Utility in the United States: gpt-oss-120b (Apache-2.0, about 120B parameters in MXFP4, United States origin) for the applicability, disposition and evidence agents, the command center's cited conversational surface and ontology maintenance; Nomic Embed Text v2 MoE (Apache-2.0, 475M parameters at FP16) embedding advisories, baselines and confirmed decisions for retrieval memory; no third party model service, nothing trained on the fly.

## How much compute and hardware does AI in energy and utilities need?

The compute follows from the models: the published energy and utilities designs size it as follows, from no new hardware to a full GPU node. Grid Context Watch, compute: One node of eight 141 GB HBM-class GPUs: 753 GB of FP8 weights, 904 GB with the 1.2 planning factor, against 1,128 GB. Grid Context Watch, boundary: Read only and one way out of the control networks; nothing writes into an electronic security perimeter, and no data leaves the operator. Reliability Atlas, compute: No hardware bought; the study runs on the operator's own in-country servers. Feeder Firewatch, frontier compute: One node of eight 141 GB HBM-class GPUs holds GLM 5.2 at FP8 (753 GB of weights, 904 GB with headroom). Feeder Firewatch, edge: Sealed industrial boxes at substations and on patrol trucks detect smoke and damaged equipment when cellular coverage drops. Turbine Island Watch, compute: One 48 GB PCIe inference GPU class card on ruggedised edge servers inside the plant perimeter, a PTP grandmaster time card and a one-way egress path; no cloud, because Pakistan has no hyperscaler region. Sovereign AI Architecture for a Grid Control Room in Saudi Arabia, compute: One node of eight 141 GB HBM cards (1,128 GB) in the operator's own data center in the Kingdom, holding 753 GB of FP8 weights with a 1.2 planning factor, 904 GB; BGE-M3 on the platform zone's rack server class; no edge tier. The node is a 4A090 class item for a D:3 and D:4 destination, so an export licence determination naming the end user is a phase one checkpoint before the order. Industrial Data Ontology for a Water Utility in the United States, compute: One air-cooled node of two 48 GB PCIe cards (96 GB, about 88 GB usable) in the utility's own server room, holding 58.4 GB of INT4 weights with a 1.2 planning factor, 70.1 GB; no inference at the stations or wells, and no data leaves the boundary. The room's power, cooling and rack space are confirmed at design and serving acceptance is gated on a measured load run. Sovereign AI Architecture for Distribution Loss and Theft Reduction in Pakistan, compute: One node of eight 141 GB HBM GPUs (1,128 GB) holding 753 GB of FP8 weights with the 1.2 planning factor, 904 GB, plus one dual socket server with a 48 GB PCIe card for the embedder and the forecaster, both in an in-country sovereign data center or colocation on the operator's own hardware; no edge tier. The node is a 4A090 class item for a D:3 and D:4 destination, so the export licence determination precedes any card commitment, with a non US accelerator class held open. A Self-Improving AI System for OT Vulnerability Management at a Utility in the United States, compute: One node of eight 80 GB HBM cards (640 GB, about 10 kW) in the utility's own data center, holding 70.1 GB by the sizing rule (117 billion parameters at INT4 times 1.2) with generous headroom for concurrent agent sessions; the embedding model on the same node; no edge tier; domestic supply, so no export control complication for a United States end user.

## Is it cheaper to own AI hardware or rent cloud GPUs in energy and utilities?

Each energy and utilities design prices three years of ownership in its Appendix A, with every price cited, against renting the same capacity from a cloud region at its deepest three-year commitment where hardware is bought. Grid Context Watch: three-year cost, About 510,000 dollars to own the reasoning node, about four fifths the deepest three-year AWS commitment (Appendix A). Reliability Atlas: three-year cost, No hardware line to price: the cost is the assessment work itself (Appendix A). Feeder Firewatch: three-year cost, owned, About 841,000 US dollars with support and power at the Texas industrial power price; three-year cost, rented, 0.88 million to 1.92 million US dollars for the same GPUs around the clock; ownership costs about the same as the deepest three-year commitment (version 2); closed model break-even, The cheapest closed model matches the owned stack at about 41 users; above that, ownership is cheaper and the gap grows with every user. Turbine Island Watch: three-year cost, About 22,500 US dollars to own the site envelope over three years (21,600 to 23,400), about the same as the deepest three-year AWS commitment for one L40S card in the UAE region, which could not keep the read one-way in any case (Appendix A). Sovereign AI Architecture for a Grid Control Room in Saudi Arabia: three-year cost, About 570,000 US dollars to own the node for three years (a range of 542,000 to 594,000: server, support and power at the national commercial electricity tariff), against about 751,000 to rent the same eight cards on the deepest three year commitment in AWS Middle East (UAE), so ownership costs about three quarters of it, and about 2.00 million on demand, against which ownership costs a little over a quarter. Renting is a price reference only, because AWS lists no Saudi region on the day read and every rented option would carry grid data across the border (Appendix A). Industrial Data Ontology for a Water Utility in the United States: three-year cost, About 59,000 US dollars to own the server for three years (a range of 57,000 to 61,000: server, two 48 GB cards, support and power at the United States average commercial tariff), against about 104,000 to rent the same card class on the deepest three-year commitment in AWS US East, so ownership costs a little under three fifths of it, and about 276,000 on demand. The lowest priced rented instance that holds the model, one 96 GB card of a different class, is about 33,000, against which ownership costs about 1.8 times as much. Renting is a price reference only, because every rented option would carry the utility's records outside its boundary (Appendix A). Sovereign AI Architecture for Distribution Loss and Theft Reduction in Pakistan: three-year cost, About 641,000 US dollars to own the node and the inference server for three years (a range of 615,000 to 667,000: servers, support and power at the regulator's uniform commercial tariff), against about 773,000 to rent the same cards on the deepest three year commitment in AWS Asia Pacific (Mumbai), so ownership costs about five sixths of it, and about 2.05 million on demand, against which ownership costs a little under a third. Renting is a price reference only, because AWS lists no Pakistan region and every rented option would carry customer and meter data across the border (Appendix A). A Self-Improving AI System for OT Vulnerability Management at a Utility in the United States: three-year cost, About 477,000 US dollars to own the node for three years (a range of 437,000 to 496,000: server, support and power at the national average commercial price), against about 544,000 to rent the same eight H100 cards on the deepest three year commitment in AWS US East, so ownership costs about seven eighths of it, and about 1.45 million on demand, against which ownership costs about a third; the region's other 80 GB class instance, eight A100 cards, rents for about 268,000 on the same plan, against which owning the H100 node costs about 1.8 times as much. Renting is a price reference only, because every rented option would carry the asset and vulnerability record outside the utility's network (Appendix A).

## Who approves the decisions an AI system makes in energy and utilities?

In every published energy and utilities design a named person makes the decision that changes the physical world; the system prepares it. Grid Context Watch: Every triage, case and risk acceptance carries a named OT security analyst; agents draft, the analyst decides. Reliability Atlas: Predictions are accepted only by a named reviewer and RCA reports validated only by a named engineer. Feeder Firewatch: Every public safety power shutoff (PSPS) recommendation becomes a decision record a named operator approves or declines; the design never opens or closes a recloser. Turbine Island Watch: The platform advises and the protection layer trips: every alert carries a prescribed action a named shift operator acknowledges, and nothing writes to the DCS. Sovereign AI Architecture for a Grid Control Room in Saudi Arabia: Agents draft and people decide, by name: the control engineer confirms the alarm triage ranking, the shift supervisor approves every switching program draft, the incoming supervisor approves the handover, and the event analysis is prepared for the protection engineer; disagreement is a first class path faster than agreement, and every confirmation is kept as memory the next shift reads. Industrial Data Ontology for a Water Utility in the United States: Agents draft and named staff decide: the engineer confirms each tag mapping, the operator on shift confirms each alarm context pack, and the compliance manager approves the monthly permit report draft before any figure leaves the room; a monthly sampled audit of confirmed mappings logs the error rate, and a 15 minute staleness alarm on the historian mirror puts the command center into an explicit degraded mode. Sovereign AI Architecture for Distribution Loss and Theft Reduction in Pakistan: Agents draft and people decide, by name: the subdivision engineer approves the ranked causes and the inspection visits, the inspection officer approves the case file, the revenue officer approves the correction, and the Grade 17 officer signs the complaint; every approval carries the approver role, the officer grade, the timestamp, the artifact hash and a written reason, known answer probes police the approvers, and every confirmed cause and closed case is kept as the record the next ranking reads. A Self-Improving AI System for OT Vulnerability Management at a Utility in the United States: Agents draft and people decide, by name: the compliance engineer confirms each applicability decision, the asset owner approves each disposition and its change request, the compliance manager confirms each evidence package, and no agent applies a patch, changes a baseline or closes a change; the acceptance rate is instrumented both ways, unflagged drafts are sampled independently each cycle, and a confirmed call becomes a precedent the next cycle retrieves.

## What does a physical AI system for warehousing and intralogistics look like?

A complete physical AI design for warehousing and intralogistics names what to sense, which existing systems to join, the object model that joins them, the models and hardware, the three-year cost and the person who approves every action. CodeNinja Atoms has published 2 such reference architectures for warehousing and intralogistics, each free to reuse under CC BY 4.0. Warehouse Twin Command (United States): An open reference architecture for system design in physical AI. A live digital twin of four distribution centers run by a warehousing and intralogistics operator in the United States: one object model projected over the warehouse, labor and yard systems the operation already runs, kept true by nightly reconciliation against the WMS, producing weekly slotting recommendations, dock and yard schedules and on-site safety vision on the existing cameras, with every action drafted for a person to approve. Physical AI System Design for a Mobile Robot Fulfilment Centre in Saudi Arabia (Saudi Arabia): An open reference architecture for system design in physical AI, built for a warehousing and intralogistics operator in Saudi Arabia that runs a robotic e-commerce fulfilment centre of roughly 400,000 square feet with more than 250 autonomous mobile robots, where owning the inference node that runs it costs about 569,000 US dollars over three years. When a replenishment cage is left in a lane during a peak wave, the design detects the blocked lane on the cameras that already cover the field, joins the stalled robots to the totes they carry and the order wave those totes fulfil, and drafts the clearance task and reroute request that the fleet controller approves, so the wave is recovered while it still matters rather than seen an hour later in orders per hour. Every model runs on the operator's own hardware inside the building, under the national personal data protection law, the occupational safety rules for automated warehouses, the civil defence fire code and export control on the GPU node.

## Which AI models can a warehousing and intralogistics operator run on its own hardware?

Each published warehousing and intralogistics design names its models and why, and every model is open-weight or no model is used at all, so the operator can run it on hardware it owns. Warehouse Twin Command: Mistral Large 3 675B (Apache-2.0) for the site manager assistant; Qwen3.8-27B (Apache-2.0) for assistant continuity at each site; RF-DETR N to L (Apache-2.0) and OC-SORT (MIT) for safety vision; BGE-M3 (MIT) for retrieval; no face recognition, no re-identification, no training run. Physical AI System Design for a Mobile Robot Fulfilment Centre in Saudi Arabia: Five: RF-DETR (small) (Apache-2.0) per frame on the edge NPUs for person detection; Grounding DINO Swin-T (Apache-2.0) on evidence frames for fallen totes, cages and blocked lanes; the Roboflow trackers (Apache-2.0) with re-identification switched off; BGE-M3 (MIT) for Arabic and English cited retrieval; GLM 5.3 (GLM-5.3 bespoke licence, 753B at FP8) for the three drafting agents, ontology maintenance and the command centre; no third party model service, nothing trained into weights.

## How much compute and hardware does AI in warehousing and intralogistics need?

The compute follows from the models: the published warehousing and intralogistics designs size it as follows, from no new hardware to a full GPU node. Warehouse Twin Command, compute: At each site, one industrial edge server with one 48 GB PCIe inference card running vision in Triton and the site model under vLLM; centrally, one 141 GB HBM class node of eight cards, on the operator's own hardware. Physical AI System Design for a Mobile Robot Fulfilment Centre in Saudi Arabia, compute: Three tiers inside the building: camera group edge units on an industrial grade octa core SoC with a 6 TOPS NPU beside the reused cameras, counted only after a dev board measurement on the real substreams; site servers on CPU for ingest, the backbone, the ontology, the embedder and the open vocabulary classifier; and one node of eight 141 GB HBM accelerators (1,128 GB) in the site data room holding 753 GB of FP8 weights with a 1.2 planning factor, 904 GB. The node is a 4A090 class item for a D:3 and D:4 destination, so an export licence determination is a phase one procurement gate before the order.

## Is it cheaper to own AI hardware or rent cloud GPUs in warehousing and intralogistics?

Each warehousing and intralogistics design prices three years of ownership in its Appendix A, with every price cited, against renting the same capacity from a cloud region at its deepest three-year commitment where hardware is bought. Warehouse Twin Command: three-year cost, About 597,000 US dollars to own the twelve cards (510,000 to 689,000) before the four edge chassis, which are priced by quotation; renting the same cards in AWS US East runs about 1.86 million on demand and about 699,000 on the deepest three-year commitment (Appendix A). Physical AI System Design for a Mobile Robot Fulfilment Centre in Saudi Arabia: three-year cost, About 569,000 US dollars to own the node for three years (a range of 541,000 to 594,000: server, support and power at the national commercial electricity tariff), against about 751,000 to rent the same eight cards on the deepest three year commitment in AWS Middle East (UAE), so ownership costs about three quarters of it, and about 2.00 million on demand, against which ownership costs a little over a quarter. Renting is a price reference only, because the design keeps frames, weights and memory inside the building and AWS lists no Saudi region on the day read; the edge units and site servers are not priced because the paper does not count or size them (Appendix A).

## What ontology or object model does a warehousing and intralogistics AI system need?

The object model is the part that makes the system an ontology rather than a pipeline: typed objects for the things in the warehousing and intralogistics operation, with properties, status values and typed links. Every published design ships its object model as hyper-ontology/1 JSON that loads into Hyper Ontology. Warehouse Twin Command: Distribution Center, Dock Door, Yard Trailer, Storage Slot, SKU, Open Order, Pick Task, Associate, Labor Shift, Camera Zone, Safety Event, Forklift, Shift Lead, Slotting Recommendation. Physical AI System Design for a Mobile Robot Fulfilment Centre in Saudi Arabia: Zone, Robot, Charging bay, Pick station, Camera view, Tote, Order wave, Shift, Incursion, Stall, Person role, Near miss, Corrective action, Agent draft, Edge unit.

## Who approves the decisions an AI system makes in warehousing and intralogistics?

In every published warehousing and intralogistics design a named person makes the decision that changes the physical world; the system prepares it. Warehouse Twin Command: Every slotting move states walking saved and move labor before a person approves it; every safety event becomes a clip a supervisor reviews, and analytics never act as a safety-rated stop. Physical AI System Design for a Mobile Robot Fulfilment Centre in Saudi Arabia: Agents detect and draft, people decide, by name: the shift supervisor approves the incursion agent's zone slow down and supervisor call, the fleet controller approves the stall agent's clearance task and reroute request, the operations manager confirms the shift agent's handover; execution is manual, every approval is logged with its reasoning, known answer probes measure whether approvers are rubber stamping, and people appear in the record as anonymous presence with a role, never as an identity.

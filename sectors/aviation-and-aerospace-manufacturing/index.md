# Physical AI for aviation and aerospace manufacturing: questions answered

Canonical: https://codeatoms.ai/sectors/aviation-and-aerospace-manufacturing/
License: CC BY 4.0
Publisher: CodeNinja Atoms (https://codeatoms.ai)

Aircraft parts carry a conformity record for every serialized piece, and that record usually lives apart from the shop floor, the metrology bay and the work order that produced it.

## What does a physical AI system for aviation and aerospace manufacturing look like?

A complete physical AI design for aviation and aerospace manufacturing names what to sense, which existing systems to join, the object model that joins them, the models and hardware, the three-year cost and the person who approves every action. CodeNinja Atoms has published 3 such reference architectures for aviation and aerospace manufacturing, each free to reuse under CC BY 4.0. Hangar Form Cell (United States): a vendor-independent dieless robotic sheet metal cell that takes an aircraft structural part from CAD to a certified, serialized part with collision-free springback-compensated toolpaths, in-process metrology and a per-serial traceability record, wholly inside the facility's own boundary with no vendor cloud. Physical AI System Design for Composite Part Inspection in an Aviation MRO in Saudi Arabia (Saudi Arabia): An open reference architecture for system design in physical AI, built for the composite repair shop of an aircraft maintenance, repair and overhaul provider in Saudi Arabia that repairs radomes, flight control surfaces, nacelle panels, fan cowls and cabin monuments for commercial single aisle and wide body fleets, where owning the three inference tiers that run it costs about 613,000 US dollars over three years. When a fan cowl arrives with a disbond at a hinge fitting larger than it looks, the design joins the bench camera, the ultrasonic and thermography scan, the serialized part's history, the licensed manual's allowable limit and the shop's own confirmed maps into one argument, so the certifying engineer sees the extent, the class, the precedent and whether the manufacturer must be asked before any work starts. Every model runs on the operator's own hardware inside the shop network under the national civil aviation regulations, the manual licences, the personal data protection law and export control on the GPU node and the site card. Industrial AI Reference Architecture for an Engine MRO Shop Floor in the United States (United States): An open reference architecture for system design in physical AI, built for an aircraft engine maintenance, repair and overhaul shop in the United States that serves airlines and lessors, where owning the inference node that runs it costs about 407,000 US dollars over three years. When a high pressure turbine blade set fails inspection and moves a module from repair to replace, the design joins the gate plan, the serialized part's location, the vendor repair order, the purchase orders and the build slot into one argument, so the planner sees which engines' dates move and by how much, the materials manager sees the chase or the used material option, and the customer is told before the date slips. The model runs entirely on the shop's own hardware under the FAA's part 145 record keeping rules, the engine manufacturers' manual licences and export control on engine technical data.

## Which AI models can an aviation and aerospace manufacturing operator run on its own hardware?

Each published aviation and aerospace manufacturing design names its models and why, and every model is open-weight or no model is used at all, so the operator can run it on hardware it owns. Hangar Form Cell: Anomalib 2.7.0 (Apache-2.0) for surface and geometric anomaly detection on formed panels, and Chronos-Bolt (Apache-2.0) for force-trace baselines, both on the cell edge box; no language model committed. Physical AI System Design for Composite Part Inspection in an Aviation MRO in Saudi Arabia: Six: GLM 5.3 (bespoke GLM-5.3 licence, 753B at FP8) for the three drafting agents (damage mapping, repair scheme, release paperwork), the command centre and ontology maintenance; Qwen3.8-27B dense multimodal (Apache-2.0) reading scans and bench photographs into structured damage; OWLv2 zero-shot detection and SAM 2.1 segmentation (Apache-2.0) at the bench; Anomalib (Apache-2.0) mapping subsurface anomalies on scan images; BGE-M3 (MIT) retrieval over manuals, records and confirmed maps; no third party model service, nothing trained into weights. Industrial AI Reference Architecture for an Engine MRO Shop Floor in the United States: GLM 5.3 open weights (GLM-5.3 bespoke licence permitting commercial use, fine tuning and redistribution; 753 billion parameters, 376 GB at INT4) for the agentic work surface, the cited conversational interface and ontology maintenance; gpt-oss-120b and BGE-M3 set aside as open work because their sources were not returned; no third party model service, nothing trained into weights.

## How much compute and hardware does AI in aviation and aerospace manufacturing need?

The compute follows from the models: the published aviation and aerospace manufacturing designs size it as follows, from no new hardware to a full GPU node. Hangar Form Cell, compute: A fanless industrial inference computer in the cell panel and an eight-card 48 GB PCIe inference server in the facility, joined one way across a hardware data diode; an OCP Time Card grandmaster orders every stream. Physical AI System Design for Composite Part Inspection in an Aviation MRO in Saudi Arabia, compute: Three tiers inside the shop network in Saudi Arabia: two fanless edge units of the octa core 6 TOPS NPU class at the inspection bench and the scan station; one 48 GB PCIe inference card on a shop server for the reader (about 28 GB at 8 bit) and the embedding model; one node of eight 141 GB HBM GPUs (1,128 GB) holding GLM 5.3's 753 GB of FP8 weights with a 1.2 planning factor, 904 GB. The node and the card are US origin accelerators for a D:3 and D:4 destination, so an export licence determination is a phase one checkpoint before any GPU is quoted. Industrial AI Reference Architecture for an Engine MRO Shop Floor in the United States, compute: One node of eight 80 GB HBM class GPUs (640 GB) on vLLM in the shop's own server room on a segregated VLAN, holding 376 GB of INT4 weights with the 1.2 planning factor (452 GB) and 264 GB of headroom for concurrent planner sessions; Apache Kafka 4.4, Hyper Ontology, Harbor and Prometheus, Grafana and Loki beside it; no edge tier because nothing on the floor is sensed.

## Is it cheaper to own AI hardware or rent cloud GPUs in aviation and aerospace manufacturing?

Each aviation and aerospace manufacturing design prices three years of ownership in its Appendix A, with every price cited, against renting the same capacity from a cloud region at its deepest three-year commitment where hardware is bought. Hangar Form Cell: three-year cost, About 202,000 to 220,000 US dollars to own the priced classes over three years, the scanner and the server most of it; the two compute tiers alone cost about two fifths of the deepest three-year AWS commitment for eight L40S cards, which the offline requirement rules out anyway (Appendix A). Physical AI System Design for Composite Part Inspection in an Aviation MRO in Saudi Arabia: three-year cost, About 613,000 US dollars to own all three tiers for three years (a range of 583,000 to 639,000: about 570,000 for the node, about 42,000 for the site server and card, under 1,000 for the two edge units, with support and power at the national commercial electricity tariff), against about 773,000 to rent the node and the card on the deepest three year commitment in AWS Middle East (UAE), so ownership costs about four fifths of it, and about 2.06 million on demand, against which ownership costs about three tenths. Renting is a price reference only, because AWS lists no Saudi region on the day read and every rented option would carry customer records, scans and manual content across the border (Appendix A). Industrial AI Reference Architecture for an Engine MRO Shop Floor in the United States: three-year cost, About 407,000 US dollars to own the node for three years (a range of 370,000 to 422,000: server, support and power at the national average commercial price), against about 448,000 to rent the same eight H100 cards on the deepest three year commitment in AWS US East (N. Virginia), a Compute Savings Plan all upfront, so ownership costs about nine tenths of it; about 600,000 on the three year EC2 Instance Savings Plan all upfront, about two thirds; and about 1.45 million on demand, a little over a quarter. Renting is a price reference only, because every rented option would carry customer, engine and vendor data and export controlled technical data out of the shop (Appendix A).

## What ontology or object model does an aviation and aerospace manufacturing AI system need?

The object model is the part that makes the system an ontology rather than a pipeline: typed objects for the things in the aviation and aerospace manufacturing operation, with properties, status values and typed links. Every published design ships its object model as hyper-ontology/1 JSON that loads into Hyper Ontology. Hangar Form Cell: Formed structural part, Sheet blank, Dieless cell, Robot arm, Forming stylus, Toolpath program, Part CAD model, Formability analysis, In-process metrology scan, Force trace, Work order, Nonconformance, Facility operator, Cell model service. Physical AI System Design for Composite Part Inspection in an Aviation MRO in Saudi Arabia: Customer, Aircraft, Part number, Part (serialized), Work order, Task card, Inspection, Scan, Damage, Damage class, Allowable limit, Repair scheme, Material lot, Technician qualification, Finding, Release. Industrial AI Reference Architecture for an Engine MRO Shop Floor in the United States: Engine, Customer, Workscope, Gate, Module, Part, Inspection finding, Disposition, Repair order, Vendor, Purchase order, Material lot, Build slot, Test, Release.

## Who approves the decisions an AI system makes in aviation and aerospace manufacturing?

In every published aviation and aerospace manufacturing design a named person makes the decision that changes the physical world; the system prepares it. Hangar Form Cell: A named facility operator releases every program, an authorized person dispositions every nonconformance, and the certified safety function admits no software verdict as a control input. Physical AI System Design for Composite Part Inspection in an Aviation MRO in Saudi Arabia: Agents map and draft and certifying engineers sign, by name: the certifying engineer confirms, amends or rejects the damage map and its classification, the design engineer approves or supersedes the repair scheme, material kit and qualification, and certifying staff sign the release; no agent classifies damage as repairable or releases a part; the disagreement path is built first and the count of engineer reports is published each week; every confirmed map and approved scheme is kept as memory the next assessment of the same part type reads. Industrial AI Reference Architecture for an Engine MRO Shop Floor in the United States: Agents draft and people decide, by name: the engine planner confirms the critical path draft, the customer program manager approves the workscope cascade and the airline notification, the materials manager approves the vendor chase or the alternative, and the quality queue checks record integrity; the disagreement path is built before the agreement path, agreement rates are logged, and every confirmed outcome is kept as memory the next plan reads.

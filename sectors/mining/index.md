## What does a physical AI system for mining look like?

A complete physical AI design for mining names what to sense, which existing systems to join, the object model that joins them, the models and hardware, the three-year cost and the person who approves every action. CodeNinja Atoms has published 3 such reference architectures for mining, each free to reuse under CC BY 4.0. Industrial AI for an Open Pit Mine Haul Fleet in Saudi Arabia (Saudi Arabia): An open reference architecture for system design in physical AI, built for an open pit mine operator in Saudi Arabia whose pit is worked by a contractor with a haul fleet of about 35 trucks of the 100 tonne class and one shovel of the 600 tonne class, where owning the reasoning node that runs it costs about 556,000 US dollars over three years. Every alarm, oil result, tyre reading, payload record and rebuild is joined on one governed fleet ontology, so the maintenance planner can ask which component on which truck fails next and catch a final drive in the workshop rather than on the ramp. The models run entirely on the operator's own hardware on site, under the Mining Investment Law and its safety regulations, the national cybersecurity controls for operational technology, the Personal Data Protection Law and export control on the GPU node. Industrial AI Reference Architecture for Tailings Storage Facility Monitoring in the United States (United States): An open industrial AI reference architecture for system design in physical AI, built for a mining company in the United States that runs one large tailings storage facility classified at a high consequence level, where owning the reasoning node that runs it costs about 456,000 US dollars over three years. When a piezometer rises slowly over three weeks after a deposition change and a wet month, the design joins the reading to the deposition that moved the beach toward it, the rain that fell, the inclinometer two hundred feet away, the radar survey from last quarter and the matching past episodes into one argument, so the tailings engineer confirms a pattern, the responsible tailings facility engineer approves the response and the engineer of record reviews the report before the quarterly review. The models run entirely on the owner's own hardware under the global industry standard on tailings management, state dam safety regulation and the engineer of record's authority over every interpretation. Sovereign AI Reference Architectures for a Remote Mine on a Satellite Link in Pakistan (Pakistan): An open reference architecture for system design in physical AI, built for a mining operator developing a large open pit copper and gold mine in a remote part of Pakistan, reached by road and air, on its own power, with one satellite link that drops in bad weather, where owning the inference node that runs it costs about 572,000 US dollars over three years. When the link drops for a day while a dozer fault, a permit clash and a late convoy all arrive, the design answers all three from the site's own memory: which work is open on each pad, which equipment is overdue or down, and which convoy carries the parts for it, with every fact stamped with when the site last saw it, and syncs when the link returns. The models run entirely on the operator's own hardware inside the fence under the mining lease, the permit to work and isolation procedures, the draft data protection rules and export control on the GPU node.

## Which AI models can a mining operator run on its own hardware?

Each published mining design names its models and why, and every model is open-weight or no model is used at all, so the operator can run it on hardware it owns. Industrial AI for an Open Pit Mine Haul Fleet in Saudi Arabia: GLM 5.3 (bespoke licence, 753B at FP8) for reasoning, the conversational interface and the agents' drafts; Grounding DINO Swin-T (Apache-2.0, about 345 MB at 16 bit) for shovel bucket tooth detection at the edge; Chronos-2 (Apache-2.0) for zero shot telemetry and oil trend forecasting with covariates; BGE-M3 (MIT) for evidence retrieval so every answer cites its record; nothing trained into weights. Industrial AI Reference Architecture for Tailings Storage Facility Monitoring in the United States: gpt-oss-120b (Apache-2.0, 116.8B total and 5.1B active parameters, MXFP4) for the pattern, response and reporting agents, the work surface and ontology maintenance; Granite Tiny Time Mixers (Apache-2.0) screening readings at the edge; Chronos-2 (Apache-2.0) building trend baselines with rainfall and pond level as covariates; Nomic Embed Text v2 MoE (Apache-2.0) for retrieval with citations; all four US origin, all self hosted, nothing trained into weights. Sovereign AI Reference Architectures for a Remote Mine on a Satellite Link in Pakistan: GLM 5.3 (GLM-5.3 bespoke licence, 753B at FP8) for the three drafting agents (permits, equipment, convoys), the command centre's cited conversational interface and ontology maintenance; BGE-M3 (MIT) for retrieval over permits, work orders, faults and maintenance histories; no third party model service, nothing trained into weights.

## How much compute and hardware does AI in mining need?

The compute follows from the models: the published mining designs size it as follows, from no new hardware to a full GPU node. Industrial AI for an Open Pit Mine Haul Fleet in Saudi Arabia, compute: One node of eight 141 GB cards (1,128 GB) in the site server room, holding 753 GB of FP8 weights with a 1.2 planning factor, 904 GB, at roughly ten kilowatts; rack servers beside it for forecasting and embedding; heat rated automotive grade SoC edge units, fanless, on the trucks and at the shovel bucket camera. The node is a 4A090.a class item for a D:3 and D:4 destination, so a BIS licence determination belongs in phase one (Appendix A.3). Nothing leaves; updates arrive one way through a hardware enforced data diode. Industrial AI Reference Architecture for Tailings Storage Facility Monitoring in the United States, compute: Three tiers on the owner's own hardware: on-device edge units at the logger network on CPU class hardware, a site tier beside the GPU node, and one node of eight 80 GB GPUs (640 GB) in the site server room holding 58.4 GB of INT4 weights with a 1.2 planning factor, 70.1 GB; the node draws about 10 kW, so a room survey precedes any order, with an in-country colocation room as the fallback; the hardware stays in the United States, so no export control question arises. Sovereign AI Reference Architectures for a Remote Mine on a Satellite Link in Pakistan, compute: One node of eight 141 GB HBM cards (1,128 GB) in the site server room on the mine's own power, holding 753 GB of FP8 weights with 375 GB beside them; BGE-M3 on the same node; no edge tier, the rugged tablets in the field run nothing. The node is a 4A090 class item for a D:2, D:3 and D:4 destination, so an export licence determination naming the end user is the first phase one checkpoint, with an in country colocation fallback if the path closes.

## Is it cheaper to own AI hardware or rent cloud GPUs in mining?

Each mining design prices three years of ownership in its Appendix A, with every price cited, against renting the same capacity from a cloud region at its deepest three-year commitment where hardware is bought. Industrial AI for an Open Pit Mine Haul Fleet in Saudi Arabia: three-year cost, About 556,000 US dollars to own the reasoning node for three years (a range of 528,000 to 580,000: server, support and power at the Saudi industrial electricity tariff), against about 751,000 to rent the same eight cards on the cheapest three year commitment in AWS Middle East (UAE), an EC2 Instance Savings Plan, all upfront, so ownership costs about three quarters of it. Renting is a price reference only, because AWS lists no Saudi region on the day read and every rented option would carry fleet records across the border (Appendix A). Industrial AI Reference Architecture for Tailings Storage Facility Monitoring in the United States: three-year cost, About 456,000 US dollars to own the node for three years (a range of 437,000 to 496,000: server, support and power at the national average industrial and commercial prices), against about 544,000 to rent the same eight cards on the deepest three year commitment in AWS US East (N. Virginia), so ownership costs about five sixths of it, and about 1.45 million on demand, against which ownership costs a little under a third. Renting is a price reference only, because every rented option would carry instrument, survey and inspection data off the owner's hardware (Appendix A). Sovereign AI Reference Architectures for a Remote Mine on a Satellite Link in Pakistan: three-year cost, About 572,000 US dollars to own the node for three years (a range of 548,000 to 597,000: server, support and power at the national industrial tariff as a reference, since the mine generates its own power), against about 751,000 to rent the same eight cards on the deepest three year commitment in AWS Asia Pacific (Mumbai), so ownership costs about three quarters of it, and about 2.00 million on demand, against which ownership costs a little over a quarter. Renting is a price reference only: AWS lists no region in Pakistan, and no rented node answers with the link down (Appendix A).

## What ontology or object model does a mining AI system need?

The object model is the part that makes the system an ontology rather than a pipeline: typed objects for the things in the mining operation, with properties, status values and typed links. Every published design ships its object model as hyper-ontology/1 JSON that loads into Hyper Ontology. Industrial AI for an Open Pit Mine Haul Fleet in Saudi Arabia: Haul truck, Shovel, Component, Haul road segment, Haul cycle, Payload record, Telematics alarm, Oil sample, Tyre reading, Work order, Rebuild, Ground engaging tool, Shift, Maintenance planner, Dispatcher, Mine superintendent. Industrial AI Reference Architecture for Tailings Storage Facility Monitoring in the United States: Embankment Section, Raise, Instrument, Reading, Trigger Level, Trigger, Inspection Observation, Deposition Event, Pond Level, Radar Survey, Pattern Draft, Response Draft, Regulator Submission, Engineer of Record, Water Balance Model. Sovereign AI Reference Architectures for a Remote Mine on a Satellite Link in Pakistan: Construction area, Pad, Work package, Permit, Crew, Worker, Equipment unit, Fault, Work order, Part, Convoy, Consignment, Camp occupancy, Incident, Utility tag.

## Who approves the decisions an AI system makes in mining?

In every published mining design a named person makes the decision that changes the physical world; the system prepares it. Industrial AI for an Open Pit Mine Haul Fleet in Saudi Arabia: Agents draft and named people decide: the maintenance planner approves each health ranking, the dispatcher approves each haul redraft, the shovel operator confirms each tooth loss, and the mine superintendent owns the command center and holds escalation authority; no agent stops a machine or changes a work order without a named approval, except the tooth loss stop, which follows the site safety procedure. Industrial AI Reference Architecture for Tailings Storage Facility Monitoring in the United States: Agents draft and people decide, by name: the tailings engineer confirms every pattern draft, the responsible tailings facility engineer approves every response draft, and the engineer of record reviews the surveillance report and approves every regulator submission before it is filed; the review board and the regulator read the section stories through read only views; every confirmed pattern and its outcome is kept as memory the next trigger is read against. Sovereign AI Reference Architectures for a Remote Mine on a Satellite Link in Pakistan: Agents draft and supervisors decide, by name: the area supervisor confirms the permit agent's shift start draft of open permits, conflicts and expired isolations per pad, the maintenance planner approves the equipment agent's work order and parts check, and the logistics lead approves the convoy agent's re sequencing; no agent issues a permit, closes an isolation or dispatches a convoy; every confirmation is recorded against the owning system's record and kept as memory the next shift reads.
